# Corley's Island

A 3D excavator game in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (three.js r186, no build step). Dig a 3 ft trench on a grassy mesa above a mountain lake (about 295 ft across: a high top with Lookout Knoll and West Rise, and a ramp down a 24 ft rocky scarp to the Lower Bench dirt yard), expose the gas main without hitting it, load the dump truck and haul the spoil over the cliff edge. Then mow the patch of tall grass with the zero-turn mower.

- Live: https://corleys-island.vercel.app (https://backhoe-dig.vercel.app still works)
- Build time: https://corleys-island.vercel.app/build.html (reads `data/effort.js`); every version in plain text: `CHANGELOG.md`
- Every version, playable: https://corleythax-creator.github.io/backhoe-dig/versions/

## Controls

The start screen is a map of the islands and the town: tap anywhere on land to start there on foot (or Carry on where I was); the machines stay where they were parked, so walk up to one and Get in. The tests are only in ☰ Tools. The map is a picture (`shots/startmap.jpg`, with `shots/startwoods.jpg` carrying the grass on past its edges) so it shows the moment the page opens, and a tap counts at once (`js/start.js`, loaded without defer): you start there as soon as the world is ready. Remake it with `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/play.js --gfx low --steps tools/bake_startmap.js` after changing the ground, roads or buildings. Loading bakes nothing in the browser: the grey and lifted texture copies come from `tools/bake_tex.py`.

Left stick swings and moves the stick; right stick moves the boom and curls the bucket. The bucket digs as deep as its teeth only when they lead into the cut (curl it so they point the way it moves, then pull the stick in); dragged flat or back first it skims a thin layer. The stick carries a quick coupler: lower it to the ground and press `.` (or Set down) to leave the attachment there, lower it onto another (the thumb, hammer and auger lie east of the excavator) and press `.` (or Pick up) to take it. The thumb grips a block of up to about 4 ft with `,` (or Grip); the hammer breaks rock and hard clay so the bucket digs it like loose fill; the auger bores a round hole straight down. Drive and Truck modes put the left stick on the tracks or the truck, and the right stick raises the truck bed. Skid mode (6) drives Dexter's skid steer: the left stick drives and turns, the right stick lowers and raises the arms and curls or dumps the bucket. Mow mode drives the zero-turn mower: each stick is a lap bar for the wheel on its side (left stick sideways spins it too). Walk mode puts you on foot: left stick walks the way the camera faces (all the way to jog), right stick looks around, Jump jumps about 6 ft (onto blocks and up stacks), Sprint toggles a run of about 12 mph that switches off when you stop, you can walk down to any beach and into the lake (deep water, you swim; Sprint swims harder; wade out at any beach), jump over a bridge railing to dive into the lake, walk out on the dock at any island and Get in its boat (Boat mode: left stick throttle and steer, right stick looks round; run it up on a beach to leave it on the sand, reverse pushes it off), and Get in (shown next to a machine) climbs into the excavator, truck or mower; Get out climbs back out beside it. Keyboard: WASD or arrows are the left stick and IJKL the right stick in every mode, except the mower, where W/S drive it straight and A/D turn it, and the number pad works the lap bars (8/2 both forward/back, 4/6 spin in place, 7/1 left bar, 9/3 right bar); 1/2/3/4/5 or Tab switch machine, Shift for fine moves, T hooks the trench box, C cab view, B blocks on/off (E or a left click on the scene place, V steps through cubes 1 to 10 ft, Q turns, Y material, \ snap mode, X pick up; in first person on foot the wheel or [ ] sets how far out it goes and a click places while the mouse is taken), Space jump, Shift sprint and F get in or out of a machine on foot, M map, N sound on/off, H help. Every key can be changed in the Keys panel (☰ menu), which also lists what each key does; the choice is saved per device. Corley's backhoe (east lawn, walk up and Get in): at the wheel the left stick drives and steers and the right stick runs the loader bucket; Drive / Dig (Enter, or the switch over the right stick) swivels the seat to the hoe, the outriggers come down and level it, and the sticks run the hoe as the excavator's arm; ; (semicolon) raises or lowers the outriggers, and it won't drive with them down. G opens the map editor. Game controllers work too (sticks, triggers for gas, LB/RB machine, X box, Y cab view, A place block or jump in Walk mode, X place block on foot, left stick click sprint, B get in or out, D-pad for blocks). **Mouse and keyboard:** a click on the scene takes the mouse for mouse look in every view (Esc lets it go; a quiet line under the top bar says so while it is taken): in chase view it circles the camera round, on foot it turns the way you walk, in cab view it turns your head; the wheel zooms. With Blocks on in chase view the pointer stays free so a click sets a block. The chase camera eases round behind a turning machine and, a while after you last turned it, drifts back behind a machine on the move (not on foot or at the excavator's controls); Tuning, Camera has the mouse speed, how quickly it follows and how long before it drifts back (`mouseLook`, `camFollow`, `camBack`). ☰ Sticks (Auto, On, Off; Auto shows the on-screen sticks only on a touch screen or once it is touched), ☰ Minimap (Off, S, M, L, XL; a bigger one shows more ground) and ☰ Messages (All, Few, Off; Few, the default, shows only warnings and answers to what you just did, and tips show on All only: `toast(…,'tip')`) are saved per device (`bhd_stk`, `bhd_mini`, `bhd_msg`).

**VR (Meta Quest 3, in its browser):** ☰ menu, then 🥽 Play in VR (shown only where the browser can do immersive VR). You sit in the cab of whatever you drive and look round for real; on foot you walk the way your head faces. ## The site

Corley's Island is a rounded square about 525 ft across (160 m), its top 24 ft above the lake, with a rocky bank down to a sand beach all round. The middle is the high top (0 ft): the trench and gas main, the tall-grass patch, Lookout Knoll (6 ft) and West Rise (3 ft). North of the dig are wide lawns and the spoil yard, at the end of a short road from the top loop, where the truck dumps. A grassy scarp across the middle drops 8 ft to the Lower Bench, a dirt yard with stockpiles. Roads: a loop round the top that passes the cliff dump spot, an 8 ft ramp graded diagonally down the scarp (about 5%), a loop round the bench and a track up the knoll. The truck can dump in the spoil yard or the bench yard.

From the Lower Bench a 1931-style bridge of three riveted forest-green Parker through-trusses on granite piers (about 340 ft, acorn lamps, a bronze builder's plaque on each portal) crosses the water south to the Family Island, a larger island about 525 ft across at the bench's height (16 ft above the lake, with a bank and beach all round like the others): the family house with a wraparound porch, a picnic pavilion with a chess table and a checkers table (walk up and Get in becomes Play chess or Play checkers: the island saves and switches to Mesa Chess or Mesa Checkers at https://mesa-games-ochre.vercel.app, whose Corley's Island link brings you back on foot beside the table; the claude.ai version opens the game in a new tab), a fire pit, a flagpole looking back at Corley's, and a roundabout where the roads meet. Its west road crosses Dexter's haul bridge, an old riveted railroad truss repainted safety yellow over its black (about 305 ft, 26 ft clear for a raised boom, machines welcome) to Dexter's Island, a flat gravel yard the size of Corley's at the same height, with an equipment shed, an office trailer, a diesel tank, parked machines (a skid steer, a dozer, a wheel loader, a semi and lowboy), light towers, a cone course and dirt piles (plan C in `docs/island-plans.html`); its east road leads to a New England covered footbridge (about 305 ft, on foot only: barn-red board-and-batten with small windows, Town lattice trusses inside, a cedar shake roof, granite piers) climbing 10 ft to Jessica's Island, a forest mesa the size of Corley's with a stone cottage and its library, a herb garden and a cliff bench; and the mainland bridge, a cable-stayed span hung from one 140 ft inverted-Y concrete pylon, runs about 335 ft south, then on as a temporary timber trestle (about 270 ft, 10 mph and 40 ton signs) and a rock causeway (about 215 ft) to the lakeside town: a boardwalk on the beach, a marina (a pier out to a fuel dock, finger piers full of boats, a rock breakwater with a little lighthouse, the Harbor House, the Dockside Grill, an ice cream stand, a boat ramp and a dry-stack rack), Harbor Square at the foot of Main Street where the causeway comes ashore (a seawall promenade with steps to the beach, and the town green with its fountain, bandstand, market stalls and the brick Town Hall and clock tower facing the water), Main Street with a block of shops each side under striped awnings and street trees, and a cross street. Behind the town the ground climbs into wooded foothills: Overlook Road switchbacks up four legs (about 1/3 mile at up to 10%) and the steeper Lookout Trail climbs straight up the face, both to the Lakeside Overlook about 160 ft above the town, a paved lot and a walled terrace looking back over the town and the islands, with the mountains standing back behind it. The town's shore runs on east and west under the mountains, and the field and woods are a broad point of land with beaches all round. A lattice-boom crawler crane on a deck barge (spud poles, a stack of steel pipe piles and a stack of plate girders) works along the trestles replacing them with steel: for each span it lifts a pile, upends it over the pier and drives it with a vibratory hammer, then swings a girder into place, and that stretch of timber becomes a riveted plate-girder span with a concrete deck and a lattice railing. It finishes the town trestle (4 spans), then moves to the north one (7); a span takes about 4 minutes (Tuning: Bridges), and the progress saves and syncs with the site. When a trestle is all steel its temporary signs come down. From Corley's north lawn a second trestle (about 430 ft) and causeway (about 270 ft) cross to the field and woods: a mown hayfield with a fence and round bales east of the trail, pine and leaf woods west of it. Every machine and the worker can cross.

## Map editor

☰ menu → Map editor, or G. The game pauses and an overhead camera takes over. Bars run across the top (Done, Undo and Redo, the view: Look, Closer, Farther, North up, Start view, Copy view, and Panel, which shows or hides the drawer), down the left (the tool groups, each an icon with its name, and Map at the foot) and along the bottom (the picked tool and its options; the selection's settings when something is picked; a status line with the spot under the pointer, the brush, the view, how many things there are and a tip). Beside the left bar a drawer holds the group's library or list; it stays open on a computer and gets out of the way on a phone once something in it is picked. Tap the picked group again to fold its drawer (or its options) away. Keys 1 to 5 pick the groups and 6 opens Map.

- **Moving the view**: two fingers pinch to zoom, twist to turn and drag to move; with Look on, one finger moves it whatever the tool (mouse: right button turns, middle button or WASD and arrows move, wheel zooms; Q E turn, R F zoom).
- **Shape**: Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth reshape the ground under a round brush (1 to 40 ft radius, strength 1 to 10). Dug holes keep their depth.
- **Paint**: lay a material from the library under a round or square brush, Hard edge (a sharp line) or Soft edge (Softness: how much of the brush fades out, 10 to 100%), down to a 1 ft brush. Worked dirt and Grass sit in the library beside Natural ground. **+ Your own texture** turns a picture of yours into a seamless tile and adds it to the library on every device (owner key). **Tall grass** paints the grass you mow anywhere (and **No tall grass** takes it away), up to about 8,300 sq ft.
- **Things**: Select and Add. The drawer holds the things to add by group (trees and rocks, buildings, fencing, site gear, yard: houses, sheds, garages, fences, farm gates, light towers, cones, barrels, pallets, porta-potties, signs, benches, picnic tables, dumpsters) and **On the map**, every thing and machine by kind with counts (All, Near the view, Selected): open a kind to see each one, tap a row to pick it and go there, tick rows to pick several, Select all a kind, delete every one of a kind, or put back the first trees or boulders. Add places them One at a time (then picks it), Stamp (a tap each), Line (drag a line: fences and gates end to end, cones, barrels or trees at the spacing) or Scatter (a brush that scatters trees, boulders or anything else at a spacing and density; Erase or Alt takes them away). Select picks one with a tap; Shift-tap, Ctrl-tap or a long press adds or takes away; a drag on empty ground (with a mouse, or with Box select on) picks everything in the box; Select all in view (Ctrl+A) picks every thing on screen. Drag a picked one to move them all. The bottom bar turns, sizes (each about its own middle, or As a group), colours (needles, leaves, house walls), duplicates (Ctrl+D) or deletes them (Delete); Clear or Esc lets go. Exact edits: East and South type one thing's spot in feet from the trench; Snap in the top bar (off, 1, 2, 5 or 10 ft) puts moves and new things on the grid, turns in 15° steps, fence and gate ends together and things beside a road at its edge facing it; with Select, a ring round the picked things turns them (drag its knob) or sizes them (drag its edge); the arrow keys nudge them 1 ft (Shift 10 ft); Line up, Space evenly and Face one way tidy several; Row makes copies in a line (along or across, end to end or at a spacing); Copy (Ctrl+C), Ctrl+X and Paste (Ctrl+V, at the pointer, or the middle of the view on touch) keep their shape, and Duplicate (Ctrl+D) puts a copy alongside. Hide (H) leaves the picked things out of the view while you edit, to get at what is behind them (Show hidden, tapping a hidden one's row, or closing the editor brings them back; it isn't saved). Lock (L) is saved with the map: a locked thing can't be picked on the ground or in a box, moved, turned, sized, copied or deleted until it is unlocked; On the map has 👁 and 🔒 on each row and for each kind, and a Locked filter. Solid things stop the machines and the worker.
- **Roads**: drag a road's points, tap it between points to add one, lay a new road point by point; set width, grass up the middle and grading (cut and filled to a height at each point, like the ramp). The drawer lists every road; tap one to pick it and go there.
- **Inspect**: Query reads any spot's height, slope, surface, whether it can be driven and what was edited there, in feet. Measure lays points on the ground (tap; drag one to move it; Snap puts them on the grid) and gives the length of the path, straight and over the ground, the start-to-end rise or fall and slope, and with three or more points the area inside (in square feet, and acres once it is big); tap the first point or Close the shape to go round, Delete takes away the last point, Esc clears. Back to start, in the bottom bar with a machine picked, sends it (or you, on foot) to where it starts the game; the Machines group in On the map sends them all.
- **Undo / Redo** (30 steps), and **History ▾**, which lists the steps by name ("Moved 3 fences", "Painted concrete", "New road") and jumps back or forward to any of them. **Map**: named maps on this device (rename this one, New map, Duplicate, open or delete the others; opening one reloads the game), Download file (`name.island.json`) and Open a file (as a new map), Copy map (a few KB of text) and Load a map, This map (things by kind, roads and their length, ground shaped and painted, size), and Start over for everything or one layer (shape, paint, things, roads).

Maps save on the device as you edit (`localStorage.bhd_map`, the open one, which Sync devices carries; the other named maps wait in `bhd_maps`, on this device only, and the open one's name in `bhd_mapname`). The claude.ai version and the Vercel site keep separate storage, so move a map between them with Copy map. How it works inside, and what to watch out for when changing it: [`docs/map-editor.md`](docs/map-editor.md).

## Screen editor

Every part of the screen can be restyled without new code: the bars, buttons, gauges, sticks, minimap, panels and menus. Open it from ☰ → **Edit the screen** in the game, or use the claude.ai screen editor (https://claude.ai/artifact/Bj6MjgPkszqEP2PJQqQ29t), which shows a copy of the screen at phone, tablet or computer size.

- **Parts**: tap a part to pick it (Tap to pick: off lets the game work normally, to open a menu or switch machine first). Hide it, set its opacity, rename it (not words the game keeps changing, like gauges), set its text size and colour, size, drag it or nudge it, its background and corners, or give it any CSS. **Change** aims the edit at just that part, everything sharing one of its styles, or every part of its kind in the same box. Usually hidden parts (menu, Blocks bar, Sprint and Jump…) can be shown to style them; hidden parts are picked again from the Changed list.
- **Several at once**: turn on **Pick more** (or hold Shift) and tap parts to add or remove them; every change then applies to all of them, dragging included.
- **Groups**: one-move sliders (button corners, button text size, gauge name and number sizes, panel corners), and ready-made groups to pick and edit like a part: every button, the top bar's, machine or bottom bar buttons, menu buttons, the three bars, gauges and their names, numbers, detail lines and meters, sticks and knobs, captions, minimap, cards and panels, messages, the whole screen layer.
- **Layout**: dock the top bar (top or bottom), the bottom bar (bottom or top), the machine buttons (left, right, top or bottom), the minimap (right or left) and swap the sticks; the rest of the screen makes room (`#hud`'s grid gets the extra rows and columns). Phones and big screens can each have their own layout.
- **Theme**: the shared colours and fonts, and free CSS.
- **Words**: rewrite the start card (title, tiles, every Controls entry) and every tip and stick caption the game shows. In a tip, `[jump]`-style names are the key for that action and follow the player's own keys; `{names}` are filled in by the game (a time, a count). A tip being edited shows in the tip bar. Published with the look. Words the game works out live (gauges, frame rate) stay as they are.
- **Tuning**: every number the game feels by (73 of them), in mph, ft, in, °/s, yd³, %, seconds and minutes: top speeds and turn rates of the excavator tracks, truck, mower, skid steer and the worker (jog, sprint), swing, boom, stick and bucket rates, bucket and bed sizes, how fast the bucket pours and spills; the truck's grip, braking, bed rates and how a load weighs it down; jump height, gravity, camera turn speed, block reach; mowing height and grass regrowth; how often, how long and how hard it rains, how fast rain soaks, puddles drain and soaked ground dries; groundwater seep, pump speed, the water table; how deep machines sink, how much mud slows them and how easily they get stuck, how wet ground must be to rut, how fast ruts cut in and how high mud piles beside them; dig speed in each soil and how much dug dirt fluffs up; and cave-ins (how deep, how close spoil may sit, how fast, the warning trickle; 0% turns them off). ↺ puts one back, Put every number back resets them all. A change takes at once on that device; **Publish the tuning** gives every device the same numbers (it is behaviour, so there is no phone or computer layer). It publishes on its own (`supabase/island_tune.sql`, the same owner key), and every device on the Vercel site loads it when the game opens.
- **Changes go to**: every screen, phones and small screens (up to 900 px wide or 520 px tall), or big screens only.
- **Publish**: edits save on the device at once as a draft. **Publish to every device** makes the look live: the game needs the owner key (kept by the owner, not in the repo), the claude.ai editor uses the owner's Supabase connector. Every device loads the live look when the game opens (Vercel site only; the claude.ai game and `versions/` keep the original look). The claude.ai editor can also bring back an earlier live look. **Copy the look** and **Use the pasted look** move a look between the two editors or keep it as a file.

A tuning is `{v:1, t, set:{key:value}}`, only the changed numbers, in the code's units (read it with `select data from public.tune_get()`, publish with `select public.tune_set(...)`). New feel numbers go in `TUNE` and are read from `TU` while the game runs; new tips go in `WORDS` and are shown with `say()`.

A skin is JSON: `{v:1, t, L:{all|phone|big:{vars, el:{selector:{hide, x, y, sc, fs, fg, bg, rad, op, css}}, css, dock}}, text:{selector:text}, words:{id:text}}` (a selector can be a list, for several parts or a group), turned into one style sheet after `css/game.css`. It targets parts by CSS selectors, mostly element ids, so **renaming or removing an id or class in `index.html` or `css/game.css` can strand a published edit**: keep them stable, or move the live skin's selectors along (read it with `select data from public.gui_get()`, fix, publish with `select public.gui_set(...)`).

## How it fits together

- **Plain scripts, one global scope.** `index.html` loads three.js r186 from jsDelivr as an ES module (an import map names it `three`, and its add-ons `three/addons/`), puts it in the global `THREE`, then runs `data/effort.js` and `js/*.js` in order as deferred plain scripts. Add-ons are loaded with `import()` (the glTF loader in `models.js`). No build step. The game keeps its r147 look on the newer version: `THREE.ColorManagement` is off (colours stay the sRGB values they were picked as, and the patched chunks make them linear), light intensities are multiplied by `LI` (π, core.js) for the physical light units, and the Ultra effects buffer is tone mapped as it is drawn (post.js). `tools/build_model.js` still builds the glTF models with r147; the files it writes don't depend on the version. Top-level code may only call functions defined in its own file or an earlier one; functions can call anything at run time. Check a new top-level name isn't already taken before adding it (see CLAUDE.md).
- **Units.** World units are metres: x east, z south (north is −z, the top of the map), y up, 0 = the mesa top. Everything the player sees is in feet (`FT` = 0.3048 in `js/blocks.js`).
- **Ground.** `baseHeight(x, z)` in `js/terrain.js` is the one source of the undug ground: the generated mesa (`groundHeight`: rolls, hills, scarp, bench, rim berm, graded roads, cliffs) plus the map editor's height layer. Three meshes draw it (plus 8 m mud tiles at 2 in wherever a machine has rutted mud outside the dig grid, `js/mud.js`): the dig grid (64 m square at 0.16 m around the trench, the only ground that can be dug; `H` is the current height, `H0` the original grade). The map is cut into 32 m dig tiles (`TILE`, named E0/W1 by S0/N1 from the mesa top's centre, `docs/dig-plans.html`); the grid covers four of them (`DIG_TILES`), its west and north edges at `GX0`, `GZ0`. Turn world x, z into grid i, j and back only with `gI`/`gJ` and `gX`/`gZ`, and test for the grid with `inDig(x, z, margin)`, never with `S/2`, so the grid can move (it follows the digging machine, `js/digtiles.js`; each tile's untouched ground is worked out once and kept, `baseCache` in terrain.js, and the tiles round the grid are worked out a little each frame ahead of it, so a move mostly copies), a 0.5 m mesh over the rest of the mesa and its cliffs (`MID`), and a 2 m mesh out to the lake. The Family Island (`famHeight`, rising from the lake bed) has its own 1.5 m mesh in `js/family.js`. Skirts hide the seams. Roads are polylines in `ROADS` (plus the islands' own `ISLAND_ROADS`, which the map editor doesn't change), looked up through a 4 m bucket grid (`roadAt`). `WALL` marks ground too steep to drive; the excavator, truck and mower stop against it. Bridge decks (`BRIDGES`, which can climb from one end to the other, and `foot` ones that carry only the worker) are not ground: `hAt` rides them, and `landIn`/`landClamp` keep the machines and the worker on the mesa (down its bank and beach to the water's edge, `shoreIn`; the dig grid can sit anywhere over the 1 m ground `OUTER_MID`, which reaches the water all round, and the beach digs as sand), the Family Island or a deck. `SOLIDS` are the buildings they can't pass through.
- **Frame loop.** `js/main.js`: input, machines, digging, spills, slumping, terrain updates, HUD, camera, render. The map editor takes the whole frame while it is open.

  **Sync devices** (☰, Vercel site only) carries `bhd_site`, `bhd_rigs`, `bhd_lawn`, `bhd_map` and `bhd_stats` (a save from before stats synced leaves the device's own alone) between devices under an 8-character code, through the `island_peek`, `island_get` and `island_put` functions in Supabase (the `island_saves` table, for now in the fuel-model project; `supabase/island_sync.sql` sets it up). Pulls on open and on coming back into view, pushes every 15 s and on hide; the first save to reach the server wins.

  Snapshots in `versions/` prefix their keys (`bhd-vN:`) so playing an old version can't touch the live save.
- **Pavilion games (Mesa).** The Family Island pavilion's two tables (`FAM_GAMES` in `js/family.js`, boards drawn by `famBoard`) hand off to the separate Mesa site (repo `corleythax-creator/mesa-games`, `MESA_URL`), which keeps its full-quality three.js r160 chess and checkers; merging them into this game was looked at and turned down (different three.js versions, the phone's memory budget, and the low-poly look). On foot within a stride of a table, `famGameNear` turns Get in into Play chess or Play checkers (`seatToggle` in `js/walker.js`, the button in `js/main.js`). `famPlay` saves the site and lawn, sets `sessionStorage.bhd_table` and loads `MESA_URL + 'chess/?from=island&back=' + this page`; Mesa's top-left link then reads "Corley's Island" and goes back to `back` (Mesa only accepts this game's own sites or localhost). On return the last line of `js/save.js` sees `bhd_table`, starts in Walk mode with no start card, and the saved worker position puts you beside the table. Both Mesa pages are prefetched once the worker is within about 80 ft of the pavilion. Navigation happens only on the website (vercel.app or localhost, not `versions/`); the claude.ai copy and old snapshots open the game in a new tab instead. Mesa's side is described in its `NOTES.md`, "Corley's Island link". Not yet tried on the owner's iPhone.
- **Updates.** The game re-reads `data/effort.js` when it comes back to the front and every 10 minutes; a newer version reloads at once if nothing is under way, otherwise it shows a tap-to-update button (`js/controls.js`).
- **Speed.** The owner's phone is the target: above 30 FPS. The iPhone speed test (☰ → Speed test) showed phones are held back by pixels, shadows, filmic lighting and ground textures more than triangles, which is what the Graphics setting trades. The scene is about 1.62 million triangles (the field grass is about 230,000; the Family Island, its trees and the bridges added about 130,000, Jessica's Island about 65,000, Dexter's about 35,000; the library's inside draws only within about 300 ft). The **Dig site test** (☰ → Dig site test) runs by itself for about 2 minutes: it digs across tile edges at every dig site, drives the excavator over the bridges to Dexter's and moves the dig grid as it goes, then reloads the page (putting the site back: nothing saves while it runs) and shows the frame rate of each part (30 FPS passes) and its digging checks. Open the game with `?digtest` (or `?digtest=low`, `medium`, `high` for that Graphics setting) to start it hands off; `?speedtest` starts the speed test the same way. The **load tests** (☰ → Backhoe load test or Excavator load test, or `?bhtest`, `?extest`, `=low`…) run about a minute from the machine's cab, driving the touch sticks as a player would (`js/bhtest.js`): the backhoe swivels to the hoe; then each arm control is checked to move the right way at its tuned rate, and six buckets are dug and dumped into the dump truck parked alongside, the camera coming out behind the truck at each dump to watch the bucket pour until it is empty. It reports the checks, each pass's time and load, the loading rate and how long a full truck takes, and the cab's frame rate per part. It also watches for freezes: each big part of the frame is timed while it runs, and any frame over 0.2 s is listed with its slowest parts, the time spent outside the game's code (the browser and graphics card), and anything drawn for the first time in it (new shaders, textures, meshes); one over 0.5 s fails the test. The first-use warm-up in `js/main.js` (`warmPre`) draws every dig and dump effect in the first frames after loading, so their shaders are built then and not on the first dig or dump. `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/play.js --eval "return ldTestRun('ex')"` (or `'bh'`) runs it headless through the game's own frame, without drawing.

## Working on it

- **Run it:** any static server from the repo root, for example `python3 -m http.server`, then open `http://localhost:8000`. Opening `index.html` as a file won't load the textures.
- **Check it headless:** `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/scene-check.js` loads the game in headless Chromium and prints the triangle count, file size and any errors. Software rendering is slow (a frame can take a second or two), so for behaviour tests drive the game's own functions from `page.evaluate` (for example `updateTruck(0.05, 0.6, 0, 0)` in a loop, or `editorOpen()` then `edBrush(0.05)`) rather than simulating input frame by frame.
- **Dig checks headless:** `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/play.js --eval "return digTestRun()"` runs the dig site test's script without drawing (about a minute) and lists each check; `ok` is false if one failed. The GitHub `check` workflow runs it after every change to the game on `main`.
- **Tour checks headless:** `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/play.js --eval "return tourRun()"` (`tourRun(true)` for first person) plays the island tour test's run without drawing and lists each check (it got on the four-wheeler, reached the dock, got in the boat, made each leg of the lake). The frame numbers only mean something on a real device: run it there from the menu.
- **Play it headless:** `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/play.js --eval "setMode('walk'); return wk.x" --shot /tmp/shot.png` opens the game with no saved site, runs each `--eval` in the page (game functions and globals; return a value to print it), and can take one screenshot; `--steps file.js` for real clicks, `--gfx`, `--size`, `--keep`; `--view "view X,Z,FACE,TILT,BACK"` opens the map editor looking exactly there (the code the editor's Copy view gives: spot in world feet, x east and z south, compass facing, tilt down in degrees, feet back), `--view-game` pins that view over the game instead. Page errors make it exit 1. It opens the page as `play.test`, so the game makes no sync or live-look calls.
- **Townsfolk:** the 32 townspeople are built in Blender 4.2 with MPFB2 from MakeHuman's CC0 asset packs (makehuman_system_assets, skins01-03, hair01, eyebrows01, shirts01, pants01, shoes01, suits01-02, suits05, dress01, skirts01, hats01, glasses01, unpacked into MPFB's user data folder). Each one's body, skin, hair, clothes and their tints are in `tools/peds/folk_cast.py` (same ids and order as `models/peds/peds.json`). `blender -b --python tools/peds/build_folk.py -- /tmp/folk [id ...]` builds them (arms posed down, baked, cut to about 7,000 and 3,000 triangles, limb weights from the game_engine skeleton), then `python3 tools/peds/pack_folk.py /tmp/folk models/peds` writes `folk.json`, `folk_hi.bin`, `folk_lo.bin` and each one's texture (skin, hair and clothes packed into one square, clothes tinted). The Townsfolk page (`docs/townsfolk.html`) shows them walking. The same 32 (same skins, hair, clothes, tints and heights) are rebuilt in the human-forge repo as rigged GLBs with a 53-bone skeleton and baked clips (ids `island_<id>`, tag `island`); `python3 ../human-forge/tools/forge.py wire . --dest models/people --tags island` copies them, `anims.glb`, `library.json`, the runtime `human-forge.js` (loaded by townfolk.js on High and Ultra only) and `CREDITS.md` here (about 19.6 MB); rerun it after the people change there.
- **Worker model:** `models/worker.glb` is built in Blender 4.2 with the MPFB2 extension from MakeHuman's CC0 asset packs ("makehuman_system_assets" and "skins02", unpacked into MPFB's user data folder): `blender -b --python tools/build_worker.py -- /tmp/worker.glb`, then `NODE_PATH=<dir with @gltf-transform/core, functions, extensions and sharp> node tools/pack_worker.js /tmp/worker.glb models/worker.glb` (1024 px JPEG textures, about 2 MB). The script picks the body, skin, hair and clothes, and makes the hi-vis vest (the shirt cut down and painted) and the hard hat; it keeps the game_engine skeleton's bone names (`thigh_l`, `upperarm_r`, `spine_01`…), which `wkMdlSetup` looks up.
- **Ship a version:** `python3 tools/release.py --asked 2026-10-18T09:15 --dh 6 --title "Name" --what "Build-log line" --trailer "Co-Authored-By: …"` from the feature branch does steps 1 to 4 below (and rebuilds the screen editor page if the screen changed); `--dry-run` stops after the merge and the scene check. By hand, the steps are:
  1. Merge the latest `main` into your branch and re-test.
  2. Take the next version number after `main`'s last entry in `data/effort.js`; add the entry (asked and live times in Central, developer hours, `tris` and `chars` from `tools/scene-check.js`, a plain description) and add the hours to the day in `days`. Run `python3 tools/changelog.py` to rebuild `CHANGELOG.md` from it.
  3. Merge to `main` so the release is a first-parent commit on `main` (a pull request merge or `git merge --no-ff`); `tools/versions.py` finds each version that way. Push: Vercel deploys `main`.
