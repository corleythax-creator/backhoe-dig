Corley's Island · master plans · October 2026

Bringing the islands down to the water

Three ways to drop every island to under 50 ft above the lake, so the cliffs become banks and beaches with room for docks. Each plan adds a temporary bridge south to the lakeside town and another north to the field and woods, built so the crane can swap them for permanent spans later. Every island keeps its theme. Corley's gets flatter and grassier, with beaches.

What the code has today

  • The lake surface is WATER = -58 m (190 ft below Corley's top, which is height 0). Every island is a mesa on cliffs drawn by isleCliff and mesaHeight in terrain.js.
  • The Family, Dexter's and Jessica's tops hang off FAM_Y (the Lower Bench height, 24 ft under Corley's top). Their buildings and roads sit on that number, so moving it moves them together.
  • Bridges are entries in BRIDGES, drawn by famBridge. Piers already reach down to whatever ground is under them, so lower ground means shorter piers for free.
  • Saves keep dug ground as a change from the original grade (H − H0), and map edits as a height layer. A plan that keeps the dig grid where it is keeps players' holes.

Plan A

High Water: raise the lake, keep the map

Bring the lake up about 150 ft instead of bringing the land down. Nothing on the islands moves: the lake simply fills the cliffs, and the last 6–20 ft of each cliff is regraded into an earth bank and a sand beach. The two new bridges reach mainland shores pulled in close.

Plan B

Double Archipelago: rebuild everything at twice the size

Start over with the waterline as height 0 and lay all four islands out again at twice the size and twice the spacing, in the same arrangement. The islands become low and green with real beaches, a dune ridge and coves. Buildings stay their real size and spread across the bigger islands, with new docks, lawns and trails in the extra room. A floating pontoon bridge at each end of the map suits ground this low.

Plan C

Corley's Lowland and a Mainland Ring

Lower the lake to 24 ft under Corley's top and drop the other three islands to match, keeping their layouts. Rebuild Corley's alone as a bigger, flatter, grassier island with beaches on three sides. Double the map with mainland shores rather than bigger islands: the lakeside town to the south and the field and woods to the north. Both new bridges are a rock causeway out from the mainland plus a timber trestle over the deep water. The crane later replaces the trestle with steel spans; the causeway stays.

In every plan

What all three share

Temporary bridges the crane can replace

  • Each temporary bridge is a BRIDGES entry marked temporary, with a list of spans. Each span is a panel truss, a pontoon or a trestle bay.
  • The crane idea (B10) swaps one span at a time for a permanent steel girder span. The deck stays drivable at every step, with one lane coned off.
  • Yellow "TEMPORARY BRIDGE · 10 MPH · 40 TON LIMIT" signs. The limit lets the lowboy with the dozer across but has room to say no to heavier loads later.

Beaches and docks

  • Every edge becomes bank, dry sand, wet sand, then a wading shelf before the drop. Wet sand reads the soil-wetness system the same way mud does.
  • A photoreal sand texture set with relief and sheen for High graphics (made with tools/pbr.py).
  • Dock spots kept flat on each island: the boat-on-the-lake, dock-and-ramp and drivable-boat ideas (M15, S05, M21) drop straight in.

Things that move with the water

  • The truck's cliff dump spot becomes a spoil yard.
  • Groundwater, the water table and puddle drainage are measured from the lake, so their tunings shift with WATER.
  • Fog and draw distances push out to show the far shores. Phones may draw less of the far shore, but the shore itself is the same on every device.
  • The pavilion and its chess and checkers tables keep working; if the pavilion moves, FAM_GAMES moves with it.
Recommendation

Start with Plan C

Plan C gives your island the full flat, grassy beach makeover, more than doubles the map with the town and the field and woods, and keeps players' dug ground. It costs about 60% of Plan B. Its first release (lake down, beaches on the outer islands) is playable on its own.

If you still want every island twice as big after playing it, Plan B's tiled ground and bigger layout can follow later. Plan C's mainland shores, beaches and bridge kits all carry over. Plan A suits you best if you only want the lower water soon and would rather spend the hours on other ideas.

Choices I need from you before building

  • Which plan, or a mix: Plan C with Plan B's pontoon bridges is easy to swap in.
  • Is the 24 ft Lower Bench scarp and ramp part of Corley's character, or can it shrink to 8 ft?
  • Which side is the field and woods on? These plans put it north, opposite the town.
  • Can old map edits on the cliffs be dropped (a copy is kept in Map file → Copy map)?
