# Map editor: how it works

The map editor (☰ menu → Map editor, or G) lets the player reshape the mesa, paint it, lay out roads and place, move and remove trees, boulders and machines. This note is for whoever works on it next: what it stores, how an edit reaches the screen, and what to watch out for. The player-facing description is in the README and on the in-game help card.

First version shipped as v60, roads as v63.

## What the player can do

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Moving the view | Two fingers pinch to zoom, twist to turn and drag to move; Look (top bar) makes one finger move it whatever the tool. Mouse: right button turns, middle button moves, wheel zooms. Keys: WASD or arrows pan (over the whole map, mainland included), Q E turn, R F zoom. The top bar also has Closer, Farther, North up and Start view (where the editor opened) |
| Shape: Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth | Reshape the ground under a soft round brush with a 1 to 40 ft radius, strength 1 to 10. Flatten levels to the height where the stroke starts |
| Paint | Lay a material from the library (or one of your own textures: + Your own texture turns an uploaded picture into a seamless tile and adds it for every device; Edit this texture changes a picked one) under a round or square brush (1 to 40 ft; at 1 ft it lays single 0.5 m cells, the finest the grid holds), Hard edge (whole cells, fully covered, drawn with a sharp edge) or Soft edge (the cover fades to nothing over the outer 10 to 100% of the radius, the Softness slider). Natural ground takes it back up, softly too. Worked dirt and Grass are swatches in the same library, but they pick the Dirt and Grass tools, which paint `ED_P` toward worked dirt or back to grass (Grass also paints over the built-in dirt yards) |
| Things: Select | Pick trees, boulders, placed things and machines: a tap picks one, Shift, Ctrl or a long press adds or takes away, a drag on empty ground (a mouse, or touch with Box select on) picks everything whose middle is in the box, Select all in view (Ctrl+A) every thing on screen. Drag a picked one to move them all. The bottom bar turns, sizes, colours, copies or deletes the selection (machines move and turn only). Several turn and size each about its own middle, or As a group about theirs. One thing's copy goes alongside it, along its width, so fence sections line up; several are copied beside themselves, to the right as the camera sees them Exact edits: East and South type one thing's spot in feet from the trench; Snap in the top bar (off, 1, 2, 5 or 10 ft) puts moves and new things on the grid, turns in 15° steps, fence and gate ends together and things beside a road at its edge facing it; with Select, a ring round the picked things turns them (drag its knob) or sizes them (drag its edge); the arrow keys nudge them 1 ft (Shift 10 ft); Line up, Space evenly and Face one way tidy several; Row makes copies in a line (along or across, end to end or at a spacing); Copy (Ctrl+C), Ctrl+X and Paste (Ctrl+V, at the pointer, or the middle of the view on touch) keep their shape, and Duplicate (Ctrl+D) puts a copy alongside. Hide (H) leaves the picked things out of the view while you edit, to get at what is behind them (Show hidden, tapping a hidden one's row, or closing the editor brings them back; it isn't saved). Lock (L) is saved with the map: a locked thing can't be picked on the ground or in a box, moved, turned, sized, copied or deleted until it is unlocked; On the map has 👁 and 🔒 on each row and for each kind, and a Locked filter. |
| Things: Add | Place a pine, leafy tree or boulder, or a thing from the catalogue (house, shed, garage, fence, farm gate, light tower, cone, barrel, pallet, porta-potty, sign, bench, picnic table, dumpster), picked in the drawer by group, which comes in at full size facing the camera; keep holding to drag it into place. How to place, in the bottom bar: One (then it is picked, in Select, to turn or size it), Stamp (each tap places another), Line (drag a line: fences and gates go end to end along it, turned to follow it, other things at the Spacing; at most 200, picked when done, one undo step) or Scatter (a brush that keeps dropping this kind while held, never closer than the Spacing to anything already there, faster for a higher Density; Erase or Alt takes this kind away under it). Each kind has a cap (`cap` in `PROPS`, `ASSET_CAP` in scenery.js); placing stops there with a message |
| On the map (Things drawer) | Every thing and the machines by kind, with counts, filtered All, Near the view or Selected. Open a kind to see each one (picked ones first, up to 200 rows); tap a row to pick it and glide there, tick rows to pick several, Select all a kind, delete every one of a kind, or put back the first trees or boulders (from `defaultAssets()`) |
| Inspect: Query | Read a spot's height, slope, surface, whether it can be driven, your edits there and what stands on it, in feet |
| Roads | Pick a road (on the ground or from the drawer's list) and drag its points; tap it between points to add one; New road (bottom bar) lays one point by point. Its card sets width, grass up the middle, grading and the picked point's height, and deletes points or the road |

The screen has bars across the top (Done, Undo, Redo, the view, Panel), down the left (the groups Shape, Paint, Things, Roads, Inspect, with Map at the foot) and along the bottom (the group's tools and the tool's options; above them the selection's settings, or the Query or road card, which take their place while shown; under them on a computer a status line: the spot under the pointer, the brush, the view, the count of things and the selection, and a tip from `WORDS`, `edTip_<tool>`). A drawer beside the left bar shows the group's part: the material library, the things to add and On the map, the roads, or Map's file card. It opens by default on a wide screen (900 px and up, 500 px tall) and on a phone opens with the group and closes once something in it is picked. Tapping the picked group again folds its drawer (or, for Shape and Inspect, the options) away. On a phone the bottom bar is one row that scrolls sideways and the status line is hidden. The selection stays across tool changes. Undo and Redo hold 30 steps. Number keys 1 to 5 pick the groups and 6 opens Map, Delete removes the selection or the road point, Esc lets go of the selection (then closes), Ctrl+A picks every thing in view, Ctrl+D copies, Enter finishes a new road, G closes the editor.

| `js/materials.js` | The material library `MAT_LIST` (append only: a material's saved id is its index + 1; built-in ones stop at 40, the owner's own start at `MAT_OWN0` 41 and run to `MAT_MAX` − 1, 71; `matDef(id)` gives an entry or a grey stand-in for an own texture not heard of yet, `matIdOk` says whether an id may be kept), the ground shader's material uniforms `matU`, and `matTexUpdate`, which builds the texture array of the materials the map uses. ## Coordinates and units

World units are metres: x east, z south (north is −z, the top of the site map), y up. Height 0 is the mesa top. Everything shown to the player is in feet; `FT` (in `js/blocks.js`) is 0.3048, so feet = metres / `FT`.

Corley's Island is a rounded square centred at (0, −5), about 160 m (525 ft) across (`EDGE` 80, `MZ` −5), its top 24 ft above the lake (`WATER` −7.3) with a bank and beach all round (`shore`). Until v116 it was 90 m across, centred at (0, 30); that first outline (`coreR`) still bounds the bench yard. The dig grid (`S` = 64 m at 0.16 m cells, centred on the origin) holds the trench and is the only ground that can be dug. Round it a 0.5 m mesh (`MID`, x −58 to 58, z −32 to 92) draws the hills, scarp and ramp, a 1 m mesh (`OUTER_MID`, x −112 to 112, z −122 to 94, `outPatch`) the rest of the island and its shore, and a 2 m mesh everything beyond.

## What a map stores

These layers sit on top of the generated mesa. Nothing about the generated mesa itself changes.

- `ED_H`: a height offset in metres, and `ED_P`: paint from −1 (grass) to +1 (dirt). Both are 0.5 m grids over the island (`ED_X0` −112, `ED_Z0` −122, 449 × 433 cells), read with `edSample` (bilinear). A map file records where its grid starts (`g`); one without it is from before v116, when the grid started at −58, −32, and `mapDecode` shifts it into place. The outer 3 cells are kept at 0 so edited ground always meets the coarse outer mesh.
- `ED_M`: the material painted on each of the same cells (`Uint8Array`, 0 natural ground, else an id into `MAT_LIST`), read nearest-cell with `paintAt` (which counts a cell only when it is at least half covered). `ED_A` says how much of each painted cell its material covers (`Uint8Array`, 255 full, as the round Hard edge brush lays; 254 also full, laid by the square Hard edge brush; the Soft edge brush lays 253 and less, fading toward the brush's rim; 0 wherever `ED_M` is 0). A cell holds one material: a soft fade only takes over another material where it covers more. On WebGL2 (nearly every device) the ground shader draws the materials per pixel (`matGlsl` in `js/materials.js`): it reads the four cells around each pixel from `matU.tMatId` and `matU.tMatCov` (`ED_A`) with `texelFetch`, blends their cover between cell centres and, where none of them is partly covered (Hard edge paint, 255), turns that blend into an edge about a pixel wide, so hard edges are sharp lines whatever the mesh spacing, rounding the cells' corners as round brushes want. Cells from the square brush (254) are drawn whole instead (each pixel takes the cell it is in, antialiased at the cell's sides), so their corners are sharp. Soft paint (any of the four cells covered 252 or less) keeps the blend. The material's colour (and its texture on Medium and High) is mixed in after the vertex colour (`matGlslPost`), turned linear first as `core.js` does to every colour. The vertex colours are left alone; the `matA` attribute only marks ground within a cell or two of paint (`matTint` returns 1 there) and drops where the ground is dug or dumped. Without WebGL2, `matTint` blends the vertex colours toward the materials' mean colours instead (crisp over about 0.25 m, scaled by the cover). `matCovOf` scales the same way, so a soft-edged Mud fade is partly soaked ground. Painted Mud inside the dig grid is also saturated ground: `digBase` keeps its cover in `mudBase` (`matCovOf`), where tracks and tyres rut deep and puddles stand in the ruts (`stampRut` in `js/dirt.js`, `waterTick` in `js/water.js`). Outside the dig grid the same happens on mud tiles (`js/mud.js`): an 8 m tile of 2 in ground is made where a rut first starts, the 0.5 m mesh gets a hole there (`midHoles`), and `edRefresh` calls `mudEdRefresh` so tiles take new heights and paint while keeping their ruts. Painting (Paint, Dirt or Grass) over ruts puts that ground back to grade, dry and without puddles (`rutErase`, `js/mud.js`), except when painting Mud, or Dirt on ground already soaked; a Paint stroke of any other material also takes dug holes and spoil heaps within the brush back to grade (the dig zone draws no paint on dug ground) unless the Keep dug shape option is on (`ed.keep`): `paintKeep` (`js/mud.js`) then records the height of dug, dumped or rutted ground under the brush (`paintD` in the dig zone, `FK` on mud tiles, saved as `pd` and in each tile's fifth entry) and the ground draws the paint as if it were level until digging or rutting moves it from there; Dirt and Grass leave dug ground alone, and Undo doesn't bring the ruts back; on Medium and High graphics with WebGL2 the ground shader looks each pixel's id up in `matU.tMatId` (one texel per cell, flagged `needsUpdate` when painting) and draws that material's texture from the array. Dug or dumped ground drops the cover.
- Trees knocked over by digging (`js/digtiles.js`) are kept out of their island's group by position (`treeFallen`, `treeKey` in `js/trees.js`) and drawn lying down; moving such a tree in the editor gives it a new position, so it stands again.
- `assets`: the trees and boulders, `{t: 'pine' | 'leafy' | 'rock', x, z, yaw, s: size, c: tint index}`, and the placed things in the same shape (`t` a key of `PROPS` in `js/props.js`; `c` picks a house colour for the buildings). A map keeps any lowercase kind it doesn't know, so things added later survive older code. With no saved map, `defaultAssets()` makes the same layout every time (a seeded shuffle; it used to be random per load). Which species a pine or leafy tree is drawn as (`treeSpecies`, js/trees.js) comes from its position, so moving a tree can change it; `assetsBuild` hands the trees to trees.js as its `main` group.
- `ROADS`: `{w: width in metres, mid: 1 for grass up the middle, pts: [[x, z], ...]}`. A road is graded when every point has a third number, its height; the ground is then cut and filled to the road over a 2.5 m bank. Otherwise the road follows the ground.

### Saved text

A map is saved on the device as `localStorage.bhd_map` and copied out by Map file → Copy map or Download file (the same text, as `name.island.json`). The other named maps wait in `localStorage.bhd_maps` (`{name: map text}`, an empty text for the starting island) and the open map's name in `bhd_mapname` ("My island" when unset); opening one stores the open map there under its name, puts the other in `bhd_map`, and reloads, so nothing else in the game knows about named maps. Sync devices carries `bhd_map` only. Start over for one layer clears `ED_H` (shape) or `ED_P`, `ED_M` and `ED_A` (paint), or drops `assets` or `roads` so the starting ones come back, saves, and reloads. It is JSON:

```
{
  "game": "backhoe-dig", "v": 1,
  "h": {"r": [i0, j0, w, h], "d": "<base64 Int16, centimetres>"},   // only the box holding height edits
  "p": {"r": [i0, j0, w, h], "d": "<base64 Int8, hundredths>"},     // only the box holding paint
  "m": {"r": [i0, j0, w, h], "d": "<base64 Uint8, material ids>"},  // only the box holding materials
  "a": {"r": [i0, j0, w, h], "d": "<base64 Uint8, 255 - cover>"},   // only the box holding soft-edged (partly covered) cells
  "assets": [["pine", x, z, yaw, size, tint], ...],
  "roads": [[width, grassStrip, [[x, z, height?], ...]], ...]
}
```

A map without `a` (all hard edged, and every map from before soft edges) loads fully covered wherever painted. Each part is optional; a map with only a few edits is a few KB. `assets` and `roads` are written once the player has changed them, and then replace the defaults entirely. `mapDecode` validates everything (sizes, ranges, types) and throws on anything that is not a map. Load a map by saving its text to `bhd_map` and reloading; `terrain.js` reads it before any ground is built, so every mesh, the dig grid's original grade (`H0`), the site save and the steep-ground grid start from the edited ground.

## How an edit reaches the screen

The editor never rebuilds the world. It changes a layer, then brings everything built from the ground up to date over the area that changed (`edRefresh(rect, full)`):

1. Dig grid: each vertex's original grade `H0` becomes the new `baseHeight`, and `H` moves by the same amount, so dug holes and dumped piles keep their depth. A vertex that was undug stays exactly equal to `H0`, which keeps the site save (`js/save.js`, which stores `H − H0`) from saving it. Then the colours (`digBase`), tufts, tall grass (`lawnTerrain`), the mesh (`updateTerrain`) and blocks.
2. The 0.5 m mesa mesh: heights, colours and dirt texture amount (`midUpdate`, rows only).
3. The steep-ground grid that stops machines (`wallBuild`), grass tufts (`tuftsRefresh`), and trees and boulders standing in the area (`assetsBuild`, `shpTreesRefresh`).
4. On a full refresh (the end of a stroke, drag or road change), the skirts that hide seams between the meshes, if the area touches a seam.
5. When the editor closes after any change: the site map's background is redrawn.

While the brush is held or a road point is dragged, the layer changes every frame but the refresh runs at most every 110 ms; the stroke's end does a full refresh. A refresh under a 15 ft brush measured about 35 ms in headless Chromium, most of it in the dig grid's colours, which is why the brush radius is capped at 40 ft.

## Undo

`ed.undo` and `ed.redo` hold up to 30 entries. Applying an entry swaps the stored state with the current one, so the same entry undoes and redoes.

- Brush strokes store only the changed box of `ED_H`, `ED_P`, `ED_M` and `ED_A` (copied from a snapshot taken when the stroke starts).
- Asset changes store the whole asset list as JSON (a few hundred entries).
- Road changes store the whole road list as JSON and the area to refresh.
- Each entry has a name `n` for History ▾: a stroke by its tool and material, roads and things by comparing the stored copy with the list just after the change (`edRoadsName`, `edThingsName`; things are named in a microtask, since a nudge is pushed before it moves them). History jumps by undoing or redoing until the list holds that many entries.

## Things to know before changing it

- Placed things block machines and the worker through `SOLIDS` (terrain.js), whose entries may be turned boxes (`lx`, `lz`, `cs`, `sn`, with `hx`, `hz` the box along the axes round them); `solidOut` pushes a circle out of either kind, and code that only needs a rough test can keep reading `hx`, `hz`. They stand on `surfAt` when the assets are built (every editor change, and `edRefresh` over their area), so digging under one in the game leaves it where it was until the next edit there. A new kind is an entry in `PROPS` and `PROP_MODELS`; keep the front at +z and the model's base at y 0.
- Own textures: an id is given by the server once and kept for good, even if the texture is changed (name, size, kind, shine or picture). There is no delete, since maps and saves may hold the id. A map using one this device has not loaded yet keeps the ids and draws them grey until the list arrives. The shader's per-id data is `uMatInfo` (colour, tiles per metre) and `uMatLS` (texture layer, shine); raising `MAT_MAX` also needs the cap in `mat_put` and the table's check raised.

- Anything new that is built from the ground (a mesh, a placement, a lookup grid) must either be rebuilt in `edRefresh` or read `baseHeight` live; otherwise it will not follow edits.
- Changing the generated mesa (`groundHeight`, hills, scarp) moves the ground under every saved map, since maps store offsets, not absolute heights. Saved `ED_H` keeps its meaning (raise this spot by so much) but may land differently.
- three.js r128 sizes an `InstancedMesh`'s colour buffer from the live `count` the first time `setColorAt` runs. The asset meshes start at count 0, so `scenery.js` allocates `instanceColor` at full capacity up front; without that every tree draws black.
- Point handles use `PointsMaterial` with `sizeAttenuation: false`, whose size is in device pixels, so it is multiplied by the pixel ratio each time the dots are drawn.
- The editor reads raw key codes (`keys[...]`), not the player's bindings from the Keys panel, so its camera keys never change. G opens it only if no game action is bound to G.
- The truck's road grip (`roadAmt`), the mower's and machines' steep-ground stops (`wallAt`), and the maps all read the live road list and layers, so they follow edits with no extra work.
- Machine positions moved in the editor are kept by the site save (`bhd_rigs`), not the map.

## Limits and ideas for next

The plan to rebuild the editor's layout (bars across the top, left and bottom) and add a full asset editor is in `docs/map-editor-redesign.md`.


- A map lives on one device (and one origin: the claude.ai version and the Vercel site have separate storage). Copy map / Load a map moves it as text.
- There is no way yet to make a map the shipped default for everyone. The simplest route: a `data/map.js` that sets the map text, read by `terrain.js` before `bhd_map`.
- Only the dig grid around the trench can be dug. Sculpting works anywhere in the `MID` area, but the bucket only digs in the dig grid.
- The scarp, the hills and the stockpiles are part of the generated mesa. They can be sculpted over or flattened, not deleted.
- A graded road's banks are too steep to drive. Laying or widening a graded road across another road can block the one it crosses.
- The Family Island, Jessica's Island and Dexter's Island, their roads (`ISLAND_ROADS`), buildings, trees and the bridges are fixed for now: the editor layers cover only the `MID` area, and the island's trees are not editor assets. The editor camera can pan over to look at it.
- The machines' start spots (used by Reset), the trench, the gas main and the tall-grass patch are fixed.
- Ideas: a road "follow the ground but smooth it" mode, a slope-limited flatten for building ramps, a shipped default map, and dig-grid placement anywhere on the mesa.
