Why they look rough today

      

        
Three bodies for 32 peopleEvery man, woman and child shares one of three 3,000-triangle meshes, so silhouettes repeat: no hair volume, no collars, hoods, caps or bags in 3D.

        
Projected photosEach look is a photo wrapped onto the mesh (the atlas). Where the photo didn't reach, colours smear, so sides, armpits and the backs of legs blur.

        
Low texture detailEach person gets a 512 px square on PC and 256 px on the phone, with no relief or sheen maps, so cloth and skin read flat in sunlight.

        
Stiff walkLimbs swing from 9 parts in the shader. There are no elbows bending, head turns or weight shifts.

      

    

    

      
The plan to make them look better

      

        
Step 1 · you, 2 min each
Photo references
For each person you keep, paste the front and back prompts (open a character above). Save the pair as <id>_front.png and <id>_back.png and send them in the chat.

        
Step 2 · Claude
Image to 3D
Claude runs the front image through an image-to-3D model on your Hugging Face account (TRELLIS or Hunyuan3D 2.1). That gives a real mesh with hair, collars and shoes, textured from the photo.

        
Step 3 · Claude
Game-ready
Trim each model to about 6,000 triangles for PC (keeping today's 3,000 for the phone), skin it to a proper skeleton with elbows, knees, neck and spine, and bake a 1024 px colour, relief and sheen texture.

        
Step 4 · Claude
Better walk and light
Real walk, idle and look-around cycles, skin that lets light through a little, and cloth sheen on High and Ultra. Phone keeps the light version so it stays above 30 FPS.
