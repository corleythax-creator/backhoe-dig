How they're built now

      

        
A real person eachEvery character is its own MakeHuman body, built from age, build and ancestry, with its own skin, eyes, eyebrows, hair and clothes. Nobody shares a mesh any more.

        
Real clothes and hairCollars, sleeves, cuffs, shoes and hair are modelled, not painted on. Colours are tinted per person, and anything a shirt covers is cut from the trousers so it can't poke through.

        
Two sizesAbout 7,000 triangles each on High and Ultra, and about 3,000 on the phone. Each person's texture loads only once someone in town wears that look.

        
Same walkLimbs still swing in the game's walk, now about each person's own hip, knee, shoulder and elbow, so kids and tall men both move right.

      

    

    

      
What would make them better still

      

        
Clothes
More outfits
The free (CC0) packs have no hoodies, jackets, ball caps or backpacks. MakeHuman's CC-BY packs do (shirts02-03, pants02-03, shoes02-03, hair02-03, hats03); they only need a credit line.

        
Motion
A real skeleton
Drive the full MakeHuman skeleton in the game (as the worker on foot does) for bending elbows, turning heads and shifting weight. That costs more per person, so it would be for the nearest ones.

        
Ultra
Relief and sheen
Add the skins' and clothes' normal and roughness maps on Ultra, for pores, seams and fabric weave in sunlight.
